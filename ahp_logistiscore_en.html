Urban deliverability assessment · 5 sections · Importance scale 1–5

Urban deliverability refers to the ease with which freight vehicles can access a road segment to make deliveries in an urban environment.

ℹ How to use this form

  1. Fill in your name and today's date.
  2. Read the importance scale reference before starting.
  3. For each pair, select which criterion is more important for urban deliverability, then choose the intensity (1–5).
  4. The Consistency Ratio (CR) is calculated automatically. If CR > 0.10, highlighted rows indicate the most inconsistent judgements — please revise them.
  5. Complete all 5 sections in order. Each section unlocks after completing the previous one.
  6. Once all sections are complete with acceptable CR, click Submit your answer.

1Expert identification

The goal of this exercise is to weight the criteria used to calculate the deliverability index. Each criterion's weight determines how much it contributes to the final score for a given road segment. Some criteria are static where they reflect fixed physical characteristics of the road network such as intersection type, number of lanes, or road slope. While others are dynamic, capturing conditions that vary over time such as congestion or roadworks.

The AHP method establishes these weights by asking you to compare criteria two at a time, which produces more consistent results than direct ranking. For each pair, judge which criterion influences urban freight deliverability more, and by how much, using the 1–5 importance scale.

Network Characteristics 3 comparisons · 3 criteria
Criteria being compared
C1 Intersection Type
Type of traffic control at the segment's intersection: priority road, traffic lights, roundabout, yield, or stop sign. Affects dwell time and flow.
C2 Number of Lanes
Total number of traffic lanes available. More lanes generally improve circulation for trucks and vans; fewer lanes favour cargo bikes.
C3 Road Slope
Average gradient (%) of the road segment. Steep slopes affect vehicle performance, fuel consumption, and safety, especially for trucks and cargo bikes.

Impediments 3 comparisons · 3 criteria
Criteria being compared
C1 Public Transport Stop
Presence and service frequency of nearby PT stops. Buses and trams stopping and re-entering traffic disrupt freight vehicle flow.
C2 Road Obstacles
Physical obstacles hindering delivery vehicle flow: speed bumps, railway crossings, and pedestrian crossings. More obstacles reduce deliverability.
C3 Roadworks
Presence and duration of active construction or incident works on the segment. Longer works create greater disruption to freight movements.

Accessibility 3 comparisons · 3 criteria
Criteria being compared
C1 Vehicle Size Restrictions
Legal restrictions on vehicle dimensions or weight (height, gross weight, axle weight, length, width). Determines which vehicle types can legally access the segment.
C2 Delivery Parking / Loading
Number of dedicated delivery parking bays within 15 m of the segment. More spaces facilitate stopping and unloading without obstructing traffic.
C3 Time Access Restrictions
Hours per day during which delivery vehicles are legally permitted to access the segment. Fewer permitted hours reduce the delivery window.
